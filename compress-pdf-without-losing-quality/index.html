How to Compress a PDF Without Losing Quality

The best compression method depends on what makes your PDF large. Text documents, scanned pages and image-heavy portfolios behave differently.

Explore free tools →

Understand the trade-off

There is no universal way to make every PDF much smaller with zero quality loss. Removing unnecessary metadata or optimising embedded objects can preserve appearance, while downsampling images or converting pages to images may reduce sharpness and remove selectable text.

Before you compress

Keep a copy of the original. If the PDF contains scanned pages, check whether text needs to remain searchable. If it is a portfolio or brochure, inspect small print, charts and photographs after compression.

Compress and inspect

Run the Compress PDF tool, download the result and compare file size with the original. Zoom in on fine text and review every page. If the output is visibly blurry or no longer searchable, use the original or choose a different workflow.

Ways to reduce size safely

Export source documents using sensible image resolution, avoid embedding duplicate images, and remove pages that are not needed before sharing. For email, check the recipient’s size limit rather than aiming for the smallest possible file.

Frequently asked questions

Will compression always keep text selectable?

No. Doc Writer Lab’s current compression workflow rasterises pages, so text may no longer be selectable or searchable.

Why did my PDF barely shrink?

It may already be optimised, mostly text, or contain images that cannot be reduced much without visible quality loss.
